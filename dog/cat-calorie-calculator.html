How this number was calculated

    Why cats need different math from dogs

    Fatty liver risk is real and fast

    If a cat's calorie intake drops too far, too fast, the body can mobilize fat faster than the liver can process it, triggering hepatic lipidosis — a life-threatening condition that can develop within days. This is specific to cats; dogs are not at meaningful risk from a similarly paced diet.

    Cats are dosed on ideal weight, flat rate

    Because of that risk, feline weight-loss targets in AAHA/WSAVA-style guidance are set as a fixed rate against the cat's ideal body weight — not scaled up or down by activity level or neuter status the way a dog's is — and are never pushed below a hard floor.

    Dogs scale with activity

    A dog's weight-loss target is calculated from its current weight and scaled by how active it is and whether it's intact, because dogs tolerate a faster, more aggressive deficit than cats do. See the dog calorie calculator.

    Weigh-ins should drive changes, not guesswork

    This page gives a starting point. Dog & Cat Nutrition Coach adjusts your cat's actual daily target week to week from logged intake and real weigh-in trends, rather than leaving it fixed at day one's estimate.

    Method: Resting Energy Requirement (RER) = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75, following AAHA and WSAVA feline/canine nutritional assessment guidance. Ideal weight is estimated from body condition score (each point above 5 subtracts about 10% for a cat). This is the same calculation used inside the Dog & Cat Nutrition Coach app.

    Not veterinary advice. This calculator gives an educational starting estimate only — it is not a diagnosis or a prescription. Kittens, pregnant or nursing cats, cats with diabetes, kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, or any cat already losing weight unintentionally should be assessed by a veterinarian before starting a diet. Never restrict a cat's food without a plan to monitor intake, because cats that stop eating even for a couple of days are at risk of hepatic lipidosis.
