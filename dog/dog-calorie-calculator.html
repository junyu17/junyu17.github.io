How this number was calculated

    Why dogs need different math from cats

    Dogs are dosed on current weight

    A dog's weight-loss target is scaled from its current weight by an activity factor — 0.80× RER for a low-activity dog up to 1.00× RER for a very active one — and increased 5% if the dog is intact. This lets the deficit track how the dog actually lives.

    Dogs tolerate a faster deficit

    Unlike cats, dogs are not at meaningful risk of hepatic lipidosis from a well-paced calorie deficit, so canine guidance allows activity to push the target lower for an inactive, overweight dog than a flat rate would.

    A floor still applies

    Even so, the target never drops below 0.60 × RER of current weight — a safety floor so weight loss stays gradual and the dog keeps getting enough energy day to day.

    Cats work differently

    Cats are dosed on ideal weight at a single fixed rate regardless of activity, because too fast a deficit risks feline hepatic lipidosis. See the cat calorie calculator.

    Method: Resting Energy Requirement (RER) = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75, following AAHA and WSAVA canine/feline nutritional assessment guidance. Ideal weight is estimated from body condition score (each point above 5 subtracts about 13% for a dog). This is the same calculation used inside the Dog & Cat Nutrition Coach app, where it becomes the starting point for weekly adjustment.

    Not veterinary advice. This calculator gives an educational starting estimate only — it is not a diagnosis or a prescription. Puppies, pregnant or nursing dogs, dogs with diabetes, kidney or heart disease, or any dog already losing weight unintentionally should be assessed by a veterinarian before starting a diet.
